# The road holds the span by default; Revoke takes it back

At rest the span is down, the road is open, the boat signal is red, and the road is the holder, with no message needed. When a boat waits, the river controller sends `Request`. The span controller sends `Revoke` to the road, and the road answers `Release` once it is closed and the span is clear. The span rises, and `Granted` goes to the river. After the boat, the river sends `Release` once the channel is clear. The span lowers, and `Granted` goes back to the road. This departs from the airlock, where nobody holds the token at rest and every side requests it. Under that scheme the road would stay closed until a car asked for it, which no real bridge does. Both sides use one lock vocabulary (Request, Granted, Release, plus Revoke) rather than domain-specific names, so there is one protocol to specify and review.
